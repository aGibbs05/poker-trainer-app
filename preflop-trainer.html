How grading works

This trainer uses a consistent exploitative model built for your game: 8-max live, $1/$2 with a 100bb buy-in cap, where opens are big (2.5×–6× = 5–12bb). It is deliberately tighter than a GTO solver, because the cap removes the implied-odds payoff that justifies loose speculative play.

Hand tiers

  • T1Premium — QQ+, AK, AQs. Always continue; usually raise/3-bet.
  • T2Strong — TT–JJ, AQo, AJs, KQs. Continue; raise or call by spot.
  • T3Good in position — 88–99, ATs, suited broadways (KJs/QJs/JTs…), AJo/KQo late.
  • T4Speculative — small pairs 22–77, suited connectors 76s–T9s, weak suited aces. Need implied odds + position; mostly fold to big opens at the cap.
  • T5Trash — suited gappers (T8s, 97s, 48s), offsuit junk, weak offsuit broadways OOP. Fold to a raise.

Open-size adjustment

The bigger the open, the tighter you continue. Small (2.5–3×): T1–3 + a little T4 in position. Medium (4–5×): T1–3, very little T4. Large (6×+, 10–12bb): mostly T1–2.

Set-mining math (the cap matters)

Set-mine a pocket pair only when the effective stack behind is a big multiple of your call. The textbook bar is ~15×; in a loose, paying low-stakes game it drops toward ~10–12× because you get paid off more often. At a 100bb cap: vs a 5bb open you have ~95bb behind (~19×) — a clear call. Vs an 8–10bb open (~11–9×) it's marginal. Vs a 12bb open (~7×) you're below the floor even against a calling station — the cap, not their tightness, is what kills it, so fold the small pairs to the big opens. From the blinds the math uses your net call — the blind you already posted discounts the price, so the same open sets a slightly better ratio.

Big-blind defense is different

When you close the action cheaply and multiway from the big blind, defending wide is correct — you're getting a price and closing. The trainer rewards this. It only tightens BB defense vs large opens or when you're not closing. Blind-vs-blind it widens further: small blinds tend to open wide and play passively, and it's the one open you have position against — over-folding there is its own leak.

Limped-pot extras

Three spots get their own rules: small-blind completes must pass the "would I over-limp this on the button?" test — marginal completes into small, near-heads-up limped pots lean fold because the capped rake eats those pots (rake structures vary by room; treat the numbers as estimates). A limp-reraise from a typical live player tends to be a premium trap — continue only with a premium of your own, or a pocket pair deep enough to set-mine it. And iso-raising limpers stays wide at $1/$2, tilting toward value at $2/$5.

Position

Tightest UTG/UTG+1, moderate LJ/HJ, widest CO/BTN (position lets you realize equity). SB plays tighter and more 3-bet-or-fold.

How a choice is scored

CORRECT the best play (+10). DEFENSIBLE reasonable, not best (+5). MISTAKE loses money in this model (+0). Sizing is graded lightly and doesn't change the action grade.
